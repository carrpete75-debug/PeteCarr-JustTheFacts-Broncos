The Broncos briefing

Pat Bryant is back, the safeties aren’t, and the money moved toward Denver. Denver is still 6–2 and a half game up on KC (5–2), and BetMGM left the division market alone for a third straight day: Broncos −125, Chiefs +110, Denver +1600 for the Super Bowl. ESPN standings Thursday morning (no games since MNF): Denver 6–2 (172–165, AFC No. 2 seed behind Buffalo 6–1), Kansas City 5–2 (167–131, No. 5 seed), Las Vegas 4–4, the Chargers 0–7. Lead news: The first Week 9 practice report was good news at receiver and bad news in the secondary. Pat Bryant (ankle, hurt Week 4 vs SF) was designated to return from IR Wednesday and was a full participant, which opens his 21-day window; Mile High Sports says Bryant called himself “100%” and good to go for Carolina. Waddle (chest) was also full. Safeties Talanoa Hufanga (ankle) and Brandon Jones (hamstring), CB Kris Abrams-Draine (ankle) and DL Eyioma Uwazurike (shoulder, a new name, hurt Sunday) did not practice; Allen and Sutton were limited for rest (Broncos.com). Mile High Report’s read: Denver could be without both starting safeties again, and Hufanga may be held until after the bye. On the backfield, Payton defended Dobbins after a 7-for-13 day: “That’s not a hot-button topic with us here … We’re good with the rotation,” while adding that Denver is a buyer, not a seller (Broncos Wire, Denver Sports). ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler reports the Colts are still asking a first-round pick for Jonathan Taylor, while suitors think a second plus another asset should do it (Sporting News tracker); the deadline is Tue Nov 10. Across the field, Carolina fired defensive coordinator Ejiro Evero after allowing 70 points in five days; Todd Wash is the interim DC, and the Panthers allow 140.7 rushing yards a game, third-most in the league (Field Level Media).

Last game · Week 8 vs Chiefs · W 28–24

Denver didn’t play better than Kansas City for most of this game. It just played the last 12 minutes better. The Chiefs outgained the Broncos 399 to 294, ran 67 plays to 56, went 6-of-14 on third down to Denver’s 2-of-11, and Mahomes threw for 321 yards and three touchdowns with no picks. Denver went three-and-out on all three of its third-quarter drives (AP) and trailed 24–13 after Noah Gray’s 4-yard TD at 1:38 of the third.

Then the offense showed up when it had to. Nix led a 9-play, 80-yard drive that ended with a 2-yard TD to Nate Adkins, then hit Waddle for two, making it 24–21 at 8:55. KC burned more than six minutes, but Singleton sacked Mahomes for 6 yards and Butker missed wide right from 54 in a steady rain. Two plays later Denver led: Nix found Coleman for 51, and Coleman ran it in from the 5 with 1:56 left. Nix finished 20-of-32, 222 yards, 2 TDs, 0 INTs. Coleman, starting the post-Harvey era, had 11 carries for 41 and a TD plus 4 catches for 63. Waddle played through the chest injury and caught a 1-yard TD before the half. Denver was 3-for-3 in the red zone, 2-for-2 on fourth down, and had no turnovers.

The defense gave up a lot and still got the stop that mattered. Rookie RB Emmett Johnson beat it for a 73-yard TD catch (5 catches, 128 yards), Rice scored in his return, and Kelce’s 32-yard catch plus Kenneth Walker III’s runs got KC to the 1 in the final minute. Special-teamer JL Skinner, who had played two defensive snaps all season before Sunday, stuffed Walker for no gain (Broncos.com), and Roach’s sack on fourth-and-goal ended it. Singleton had 14 tackles and a sack, Surtain had 8 tackles in his return, and KC was hit with 11 penalties for 105 yards.

The concerns are real. A 2-of-11 third-down rate is the same problem as Arizona (3-of-11). The run game managed 77 yards at 3.3 a carry, with Dobbins at 7 for 13. The secondary got thinner during the game, with Abrams-Draine (ankle) and Brandon Jones (hamstring) both leaving and Hufanga already out. Carolina is a softer opponent, but Wednesday’s report showed Hufanga, Jones and Abrams-Draine all still sidelined, so the back end that held on Sunday may be the one that plays again.

No fresh stadium item overnight (checked Thu a.m.). The newest real piece is still the Oct 29 Broncos Wire report that Denver is a finalist to host an NCAA men’s Final Four between 2032 and 2036 in the new retractable-roof stadium. Nothing new on the city process. The main comment window on the Burnham Yard Small Area Plan draft closed Oct 28; per the city’s plan page, comments received before Wednesday, Dec 2 go to the Denver Planning Board with the staff report, and later ones go to City Council (vote expected early 2027; Denver7 says January). The Broncos’ rezoning application follows adoption, and the team still targets spring 2027 construction and a 2031 opening.
