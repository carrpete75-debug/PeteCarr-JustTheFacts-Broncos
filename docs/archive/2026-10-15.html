Last game · Week 5 @ Chargers · W 23–17 (plus tonight’s angle below)

The defense won this game, and the offense almost gave it back. Denver led 10–3 at the half, already more than the 3 first-half points it scored in its previous two games combined, and the touchdown was a gift. On the Chargers’ opening series, Talanoa Hufanga punched the ball out of Charlie Kolar’s hands and Brandon Jones recovered at the LAC 40. Four plays later, after a Chargers face-mask penalty, J.K. Dobbins scored from 2 yards out for his first rushing TD of the season. A 14-play, 60-yard march ended in a 34-yard Wil Lutz field goal. Then the third quarter got away. Los Angeles, starting four rookie offensive linemen, went 73 yards in 14 plays and 9:28 to tie it on Herbert’s 7-yard TD to Hayden Rucci. With 1:15 left in the quarter, Bo Nix fumbled on a scramble at his own 40, Tuli Tuipulotu recovered, and Kimani Vidal ran 42 yards for a touchdown on the next snap. That made it 17–10 Chargers.

The offense was pitiful again, just in a new way. In Sean Payton’s first game back calling plays, Nix went 16-of-27 for 123 yards: 4.6 yards per attempt, a 27.6 QBR, and worse than last week’s 5.1. Denver was 1-of-12 on third down, gained 224 total yards, and scored on 2 of 5 red-zone trips. Six of its 18 first downs came on penalties. Two Chargers pass-interference calls alone were worth 46 yards. Evan Engram led the team with 4 catches for 55 yards. Jaylen Waddle caught 2 of 5 targets for 19, and Courtland Sutton had 2 for 18 plus the touchdown. The run game was the one thing that worked: 29 carries for 106 yards, with Dobbins at 18 for 68. Early in the fourth quarter, Marvin Mims Jr.’s 26-yard punt return set Denver up at the LAC 27. The Broncos gained 3 yards, and Lutz missed a 42-yarder wide left. Payton said it plainly: “We really struggled on third down offensively.”

The defense stepped up. With 10:33 left, Jim Harbaugh went for it on fourth-and-1 at his own 41, and Que Robinson stuffed Herbert for no gain. Harbaugh said afterward, “It was the wrong decision… Can’t think of a time where I’ve ever been more mad at myself.” Five plays later, helped by a roughing-the-passer penalty, Nix hit Sutton from 5 yards to tie it at 17. On the next Chargers series, Zach Allen strip-sacked Herbert and Robinson recovered at the LAC 23. Denver got to the 1 and then kicked a 19-yard field goal on fourth-and-goal (20–17, 5:07). It was the safe call, and against a better team it would cost them. D.J. Jones sacked Herbert on the next drive, and Brandon Jones intercepted him on fourth-and-6. Denver took two delay-of-game penalties on the next drive, the second on fourth-and-2 at the 13, and Lutz hit from 36 with 1:08 left (23–17). Ja’Quan McMillian’s sack ended it. Denver had 5 sacks (Nik Bonitto 2) and 3 takeaways, and Herbert finished 14-of-24 for 121 yards, 2.8 net yards per pass. That came against a secondary missing Surtain and Moss, with Jahdae Barron and Kris Abrams-Draine outside.

What has to change tonight: this was a winless team playing without both starting tackles, Derwin James, and Quentin Johnston, and Denver needed a fourth-down gamble, two fumbles, and an interception to beat it by six. Seattle is the defending champion and has scored at least 29 points in four straight games, including Sunday’s 32–29 loss to San Francisco. Going 1-of-12 on third down won’t beat the Seahawks. Neither will settling for a field goal from the 1, or a quarterback averaging under 5 yards a throw. The final report makes the secondary math blunt: Surtain and Hufanga are Out, and Moss is only Questionable on ribs, so Barron, Abrams-Draine, McMillian, and Devon Key may be the ones dealing with Smith-Njigba, who is off the report and has 49% of Seattle’s receiving yards. Denver has to stay on the field, get Waddle and Sutton the ball past the sticks, and stop counting on three takeaways.

Tonight’s angle: Seattle has turned it over eight times in its last three games, including five Darnold interceptions, and threw a first-possession pick in each of the last two weeks. Its defense has forced seven takeaways in the last two weeks. Denver has 7 takeaways in five games, and all three of its wins needed a fourth-quarter rally. Both teams are 16–3 since the start of 2024, Denver at home and Seattle on the road. Something gives at Mile High, with both teams in throwbacks.

Postgame: Payton: “We took it away three times defensively and we forced a fourth down on a QB sneak. So essentially four big game changers.” Nix on the takeaways: “I don’t think we win the game without those turnovers… probably two of them we were in a good spot to score touchdowns and we didn’t, so we need to clean that up.” Nix called the play-calling switch “smooth” and “a collaboration.” Attendance at SoFi was 69,293.

New stadium news

newstadium.denverbroncos.com

Fresh since yesterday’s briefing: on Wednesday (Oct 14), Denver released the public review draft of the Burnham Yard Small Area Plan, a 61-page document with 30+ policy recommendations. That is the draft yesterday’s page said had not posted yet. Comments are due Oct 28, the Planning Board reviews it later this year, and City Council adoption is targeted for early 2027. Background: Burnham Yard is the preferred site for a retractable-roof stadium and mixed-use district, targeting a 2031 opening with groundbreaking aimed at spring 2027.

  • Denver releases draft small area plan for Broncos’ Burnham Yard site Denver Post · Oct 14, 2026 · NEW

    This is a nonbinding guide, but a key approval milestone. The Planning Board reviews it in the next two months, and the Council votes in early 2027, slightly behind the city’s original timeline. Rezoning, the development agreement, an urban renewal district, remediation, the Denver Water facility, and the community benefits agreement are still open. Construction still has to start by next spring to make 2031.

  • The first draft of the Burnham Yard Small Area Plan is out. What does that mean? Denverite · Oct 14, 2026 · NEW

    Takeaways from the 61-page draft: housing and displacement protections, environmental cleanup of the old railyard, and transit anchored by the 10th & Osage and Auraria West stations. The Broncos reserved space for a Front Range rail special-event stop.

  • Housing and transit anchor vision for Broncos stadium site Axios Denver · Oct 14, 2026 · NEW

    30+ recommendations for the 58-acre site. The Broncos said they are “pleased to see this part of the city’s process progressing forward,” with “a number of details to be determined.”

  • City release: Burnham Yard Small Area Plan draft available for public review Denver CPD · Oct 14, 2026 · NEW

    Five guiding principles cover history and culture, protecting neighborhoods, environmental resilience, economic opportunity, and connections. Public comments are due Wed Oct 28. Hard copies are at La Alma Rec Center and the Emhoolah Jr. Branch Library.

  • Will the Broncos host a Super Bowl after Burnham Yard opens? Denver Post · Oct 2, 2026

    Mayor Mike Johnston says the goal is the 2033 Super Bowl, the first one Denver could host if the stadium opens for the 2031 season.

  • Neighbors: Lot M / Denver Water piece of stadium plan a non-starter Denver7 · recent

    Neighbors object to moving a Denver Water emergency facility to Lot M. Councilwoman Torres told the Post Wednesday she is optimistic about a resolution soon.

  • Broncos hope to start stadium construction by spring 2027 Denverite · Aug 13, 2026

    Leech on why a 2027 start is critical for 2031. Also covers the small-area plan, large development review, and the DURA urban-renewal process.

  • Broncos share updated Burnham Yard master plan renderings denverbroncos.com · Aug 4, 2026

    Official update with street-level concepts for Osage Station Park, Village Center, Mile High Line, and Turntable Green.
