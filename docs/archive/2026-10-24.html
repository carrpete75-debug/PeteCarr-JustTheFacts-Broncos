Last game · Week 6 vs Seahawks · W 17–14 OT

The Broncos are atrocious on offense and keep finding ways to win anyway. Against the defending Super Bowl champions on Thursday night, Denver put up 168 total yards — less than half of Seattle’s 353 — and still walked out of Empower Field with a 17–14 overtime win. Ja’Quan McMillian intercepted Sam Darnold on Seattle’s first OT drive, and Wil Lutz drilled a 53-yard field goal with 4:53 left in the extra period. Attendance was 76,973.

The first half was the only time the offense looked competent. After Myers opened with a 55-yard field goal, Denver answered with a 16-play, 74-yard march capped by J.K. Dobbins’s 4-yard run, then RJ Harvey punched in from a yard out to make it 14–3 at the half. After that: three first downs the rest of the way in regulation, Bo Nix finishing 17-of-27 for 107 yards (4.0 per attempt, QBR 29.0), Dobbins 17 carries for 41, and a 3-of-14 third-down night. Nix said it plainly: “I don’t know why we can’t really push the ball vertically right now… we’re struggling to find those explosive plays.”

The defense was magnificent again, and it had to be. Without Pat Surtain II and Talanoa Hufanga (both Out), with Riley Moss active on ribs but barely on the stat sheet (1 tackle), Denver still hit Darnold constantly and finished with 5 sacks (McMillian, Que Robinson, Jonah Elliss, plus half-sacks for Bonitto, Allen, Strnad, Roach) and 2 interceptions (Abrams-Draine in the fourth, McMillian in OT). Darnold went 27-of-51 for 278, 0 TD, 2 INT. Jaxon Smith-Njigba still piled up 12 catches for 134. Seattle scored 11 unanswered in the final 5½ minutes of regulation — Holani’s 1-yard TD plus a 2-point pass, then Myers’s 34-yarder with 3 seconds left after a 31-yard Shaheed punt return — because Denver’s Abrams-Draine pick with 1:25 left was followed by a three-and-out that burned only 15 seconds.

Sean Payton: “Defensively, we were magnificent,” and also, “I’m a little bit of a realist to know that 4–2 could easily be 2–4.” That is the story of this team through six weeks. Two straight one-score wins over bad-or-banged-up opponents still leave an offense averaging under 5 yards a throw and a secondary that cannot keep borrowing turnovers forever. Arizona is allowing an NFL-worst 8.6 yards per pass. If Nix can’t find explosives against that, the mini-bye was wasted. Otherwise Denver goes back to living on McMillian and Lutz.

Postgame notes: Macdonald on Darnold: “The quarterback’s getting hit way too much.” Darnold credited Vance Joseph’s defense. Myers missed one of three field-goal tries. Shaheed lost a fumble. Denver’s red zone was 2-of-2; Seattle’s was 1-of-2.
