2–23rd in AFC West

Last game · Week 4 @ 49ers · L 24–14

The defense did enough to win this one, and the offense wasted it. Denver held an unbeaten San Francisco team to three points on its first seven possessions. Purdy got stuffed on a fourth-down sneak, and Zach Allen blocked Eddy Pineiro’s 54-yard try on the last play of the half. The Broncos still went into the locker room tied 3–3, because their only first-half points came on a 13-play, 55-yard march that ended in a Wil Lutz 52-yarder. Then one play decided the game. Midway through the third quarter, Siran Neal forced a Marvin Mims Jr. fumble on a punt return and recovered it at the Denver 28 (the call was upheld on review). The 49ers went 28 yards in nine plays, converting a fourth-and-1 along the way, and Purdy hit Deebo Samuel from 1 yard out to make it 10–3. San Francisco never trailed again.

The offense was pitiful. Bo Nix went 25-of-42 for 214 yards, 5.1 yards per attempt, 1 TD, 0 INT, and a 43.8 QBR. The leading receiver was RB RJ Harvey (10 catches, 68 yards), and SI counted 14 completions to running backs. Courtland Sutton caught 1 of 6 targets for 4 yards, and Evan Engram caught 1 of 5 for 6. The run game managed 82 yards on 22 carries (3.7 a carry; Dobbins 11 for 40), so the 49ers could crowd the box and sit on the short throws. Denver was flagged 7 times for 44 yards to San Francisco’s 1 for 5. Down 10–3 on fourth-and-2 at the SF 22 to open the fourth quarter, the Broncos kicked a 40-yard field goal. San Francisco answered with a 74-yard drive capped by Christian McCaffrey’s 1-yard run (17–6).

Nix found some late-game life again: 70 yards in eight plays, a 13-yard TD to Jaylen Waddle, and Tyler Badie’s two-point run made it 17–14 with 4:05 left. Five plays later, George Kittle took a screen behind the line and outran Talanoa Hufanga 56 yards to the end zone (24–14, 2:15 left). Lutz’s 58-yard try sailed wide right at 1:18. By then the secondary was makeshift. Pat Surtain II went down without contact covering Mike Evans late in the second quarter (right ankle), and Riley Moss (ribs) was also out by halftime, so Jahdae Barron, Kris Abrams-Draine, and Ja’Quan McMillian finished at corner. The 49ers then scored touchdowns on three straight possessions (28, 74, and 69 yards). Purdy finished 19-of-30 for 218 yards, 2 TD, 0 INT, and wasn’t sacked. Denver had zero sacks, and Evans played through his ribs injury for 5 catches and 76 yards. WR Pat Bryant was carted off with an ankle injury in the third quarter after a 30-yard catch.

New Thursday: Payton is done waiting on the offense. He’s taking play-calling back from first-year OC Davis Webb (the change was told to Webb on Monday), and Denver comes into Sunday sixth-worst in the league in both points (18.5) and yards (280.3) per game, per Broncos Wire, versus 23.6 and 342.6 last year with Payton calling it. “I know when I have a future head coach,” Payton said of Webb, but four weeks of checkdowns and first-half shutouts made the call for him.

What has to change at SoFi: Denver has scored 3 first-half points in its last two games combined, and it can’t keep spotting good teams a whole half. Throw the ball downfield. A 5.1 yards-per-attempt game plan built on checkdowns lets defenses stack the line. Score touchdowns in the middle of the field instead of kicking field goals on fourth-and-short. Hold onto the ball on returns. Get after the quarterback: Justin Herbert has been sacked 12 times in four games, and the Chargers practiced Wednesday without either starting tackle (Slater, Alt) or LG Penning. Los Angeles is 0–4 and banged up, so this is the get-right game. The cornerback problem is now official. Surtain, Moss, and Bryant sat out both Wednesday and Thursday, and Payton brushed off the IR question: “That’s jumping way ahead. I don’t think so, and if they were I wouldn’t say so, but I don’t think so.” ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler reported Wednesday that Denver is bracing for Surtain to miss two to four weeks, and Moss could miss a game or two. That puts 2025 first-rounder Jahdae Barron and Kris Abrams-Draine outside, with Ja’Quan McMillian in the slot, against a Chargers receiver group that may itself be missing McConkey. “They’re savvy players, they both have good football instincts and IQ,” Payton said. Barron has allowed 5 catches on 6 targets this year, per PFF, in a 70-snap sample.

Postgame: Payton: “We just didn’t have enough offense to get a lead and then go beyond that.” Nix: “We just made it be a four-quarter game for too long. We didn’t help them out… I definitely don’t think we hit the panic button.” Attendance 70,776 at Levi’s Stadium.
