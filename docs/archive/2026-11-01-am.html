The Broncos briefing

First place is on the line today, and Kansas City’s secondary got thinner overnight. On Saturday the Chiefs put CB L’Jarius Sneed (knee) on injured reserve and downgraded rookie CB Mansoor Delane (shoulder) to OUT, signing Melvin Smith from the practice squad and elevating DE Tyreke Smith. Rookie DE R Mason Thomas (personal) is traveling separately and is still listed questionable. Denver moved RJ Harvey (knee) to injured reserve, signed S Taylor Rapp to the 53 to start for Hufanga again, and elevated RB Cody Schrader and OL Gavin Ortega. Schefter and Rapoport both report Waddle (chest) is trending toward playing; Allen, Bolles and Brandon Jones remain game-time calls, with inactives about 12:55 PM MST. The markets finally twitched toward Denver: DraftKings still has KC −1.5 and 40.5, but the moneyline tightened to KC −115 / DEN −105 (was −118 / −102), and Kalshi moved for the first time in five days, DEN 48→49¢, KC 53→51¢. NWS: 63°F, mostly cloudy, about 11% rain at kickoff.

Record

5–22nd in AFC West · 0.5 GB

Still a half game behind Kansas City (5–1), and today decides first place. No NFL games were played Saturday, so ESPN’s standings this morning are unchanged: KC 5–1 (143–103, AFC No. 1 seed), Denver 5–2 (144–141, No. 5 seed), Las Vegas 3–4 (2.5 GB), the Chargers 0–6 (5 GB). Denver is 3–0 at home, 2–2 on the road, 1–1 in the division, and has won three straight. KC is 3–0 in the division. A Denver win means 6–2, a half game ahead, and a 1–1 season-series split after the 31–10 Week 1 loss at Arrowhead. A loss puts Denver 1½ back and KC 4–0 in the division. It’s also Payton’s 300th regular-season game as a head coach (189–110, Denver Gazette).

Lead news · Saturday’s roster moves: KC’s cornerback room took two hits. The Chiefs placed L’Jarius Sneed (knee) on injured reserve (out at least four games; he had played 63% of defensive snaps, per Arrowhead Pride) and downgraded rookie Mansoor Delane (shoulder) to OUT. Delane, the No. 6 overall pick, has 27 tackles, an interception and five passes defensed (PFT). CB Melvin Smith was signed to the 53 and DE Tyreke Smith elevated; the KC Star expects Kristian Fulton to start for Delane and Chris Roland-Wallace to see more snaps. R Mason Thomas (personal) is traveling to Denver separately and Reid expects him to play. For Denver, RJ Harvey (knee) went to injured reserve (out at least four games, eligible again Week 13 vs Miami, per Broncos.com), and Taylor Rapp was promoted to the 53. He had used all three practice-squad elevations and has played 69% of the defensive snaps the last two weeks (Denver Sports). RB Cody Schrader and rookie OL Gavin Ortega were elevated; Schrader is RB3 behind Dobbins and Coleman. Mile High Report reads the lack of a receiver elevation as a good sign for Waddle, and Sunday morning Schefter and Rapoport both reported Waddle trending toward playing. Allen (hamstring), Bolles (hip) and Brandon Jones (hamstring) are still questionable with no new reports. Inactives are due about 12:55 PM MST.

Last game · Week 7 @ Cardinals · W 30–16

For one Sunday the Broncos’ offense found the explosive plays it had been missing. A week after putting up 168 yards against Seattle, Denver gained 377 yards on 56 plays (6.7 per play) against the NFL’s worst pass defense. Most of that came from three snaps: Nix’s 62-yard scramble, Harvey’s 52-yard screen and Mims’ 47-yard catch-and-run. Those three plays account for 161 yards. The other 53 plays averaged about 4 yards each. That’s still the story of this offense, but it took what Arizona gave it, and the Gazette notes Payton’s first two games calling plays averaged 196 yards.

The details. Denver led 6–0 after the first quarter on Lutz field goals of 48 and 43 yards, in a quarter with 10 penalties, five per team. Arizona tied it at 6 on two Ryland short field goals. Nix, who had 37 rushing yards in his first six games, found nobody open and took off down the left sideline for 62 yards, giving Denver a 13–6 halftime lead. Harvey’s screen made it 20–6 at 10:26 of the third. Arizona answered with a 7-yard Trey McBride TD. On the next drive, Nix hit Mims for 47, and J.K. Dobbins scored from a yard out on the final play of the third quarter, 27–13. Jonah Coleman, back from IR, was the most efficient back: 9 carries for 54 plus 2 catches for 15. As a team Denver ran for 159 yards at 6.1 a carry.

The defense bent but didn’t break. Arizona ran 75 plays, had 24 first downs and held the ball for 33:18, and Jacoby Brissett threw for 272 yards (27-of-48). McBride caught 10 passes for 111 yards and Denver had no answer for him. But the Cardinals went 1-of-5 in the red zone and 5-of-17 on third down. They settled for field goals on two first-half trips inside the Denver 10, and their last-ditch drive was stopped at the 1. Denver had 3 sacks (Singleton, Roach, Sai’vion Jones), 12 QB hits (AP) and 6 tackles for loss, and Alex Singleton made 19 tackles. There were no takeaways, so this time the defense won without forcing turnovers.

The problems: 11 penalties for 114 yards, a season high (Gazette), a 3-of-11 third-down rate, and only one TD in two red-zone trips. Do that against Kansas City and you lose. What has to change today: clean up the penalties, finish drives, and stop the tight end. Kelce is off the injury report, so that job is a full one. Seattle held the Chiefs to 213 yards by going 11-of-16 on third down and keeping Mahomes on the sideline; Denver went 3-of-11. That’s the gap. Take away the three long plays in Arizona and Denver gained about 216 yards on 53 snaps, roughly 4 a play (Yahoo via Mile High Report). And one of those three explosive plays, Harvey’s 52-yard screen, is out of the lineup Sunday. The final report cuts both ways. Surtain is back, and the secondary that just let McBride catch 10 balls needed him. But Hufanga is out and Brandon Jones is questionable, so Denver could face Mahomes with Devon Key and Taylor Rapp at safety. Waddle, Allen and Bolles are all game-time calls. Kansas City, meanwhile, gets Rice back for the first time since Week 4, but it will line up without Delane and Sneed, two of its top three corners, which is the opening Denver’s receivers didn’t have in Week 1, when Waddle caught one pass for 2 yards.
