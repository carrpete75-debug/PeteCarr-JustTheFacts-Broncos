The Broncos briefing

Denver wakes up in first place and, for the first time this season, as the betting favorite to win the AFC West. After Sunday’s 28–24 comeback over Kansas City, BetMGM flipped the division market overnight: Denver −125 (was +155), KC +110 (was −190), and Denver’s Super Bowl price shortened from +1800 to +1600. The Broncos are 6–2, a half game up on KC (5–2) and the AFC’s No. 2 seed. Week 9 is at Carolina (3–4): DraftKings holds Denver −3, the total dipped to 43.5, Kalshi has DEN 60¢, and NWS calls for a mostly sunny 70°F Sunday in Charlotte. The injury questions are in the secondary: CB Kris Abrams-Draine (ankle) and S Brandon Jones (hamstring), with no official update yet. Payton’s Monday press conference is next.

6–21st in AFC West · 0.5 game up

Lead news: The betting market moved hard toward Denver overnight (see the AFC West odds tile). AP notes the Broncos are only 10-for-48 on third down over their four-game win streak, so that’s the number to fix at Carolina. Roach’s fourth-down sack on the final play preserved the win. On KC’s last drive, a Chiefs touchdown with four seconds left was wiped out by a false start (Broncos Wire). AP notes Denver has now won 15 straight one-score games over two seasons, Mahomes fell to 80–3 in his career when leading by double digits in the fourth, and Nix’s 15th career game-winning drive passes Dak Prescott for the most by a QB in his first three seasons since the merger. Payton picked up his 200th career win.

Last game · Week 8 vs Chiefs · W 28–24

Denver didn’t play better than Kansas City for most of this game. It just played the last 12 minutes better. The Chiefs outgained the Broncos 399 to 294, ran 67 plays to 56, went 6-of-14 on third down to Denver’s 2-of-11, and Mahomes threw for 321 yards and three touchdowns with no picks. Denver went three-and-out on all three of its third-quarter drives (AP) and trailed 24–13 after Noah Gray’s 4-yard TD at 1:38 of the third.

Then the offense showed up when it had to. Nix led a 9-play, 80-yard drive that ended with a 2-yard TD to Nate Adkins, then hit Waddle for two, making it 24–21 at 8:55. KC burned more than six minutes, but Singleton sacked Mahomes for 6 yards and Butker missed wide right from 54 in a steady rain. Two plays later Denver led: Nix found Coleman for 51, and Coleman ran it in from the 5 with 1:56 left. Nix finished 20-of-32, 222 yards, 2 TDs, 0 INTs. Coleman, starting the post-Harvey era, had 11 carries for 41 and a TD plus 4 catches for 63. Waddle played through the chest injury and caught a 1-yard TD before the half. Denver was 3-for-3 in the red zone, 2-for-2 on fourth down, and had no turnovers.

The defense gave up a lot and still got the stop that mattered. Rookie RB Emmett Johnson beat it for a 73-yard TD catch (5 catches, 128 yards), Rice scored in his return, and Kelce’s 32-yard catch plus Kenneth Walker III’s runs got KC inside the Denver 5 in the final minute. It held there, and Roach’s sack on fourth-and-goal ended it. Singleton had 14 tackles and a sack, Surtain had 8 tackles in his return, and KC was hit with 11 penalties for 105 yards.

The concerns are real. A 2-of-11 third-down rate is the same problem as Arizona (3-of-11). The run game managed 77 yards at 3.3 a carry, with Dobbins at 7 for 13. The secondary got thinner during the game, with Abrams-Draine (ankle) and Brandon Jones (hamstring) both leaving and Hufanga already out. Carolina is a softer opponent, but Denver needs updates on its safeties and corners this week.

New stadium news

newstadium.denverbroncos.com

No fresh stadium item overnight (checked Mon a.m.). The newest real piece is still Thursday’s Broncos Wire report that Denver is a finalist to host an NCAA men’s Final Four between 2032 and 2036 in the new retractable-roof stadium. Nothing new on the city process. The main comment window on the Burnham Yard Small Area Plan draft closed Oct 28; per the city’s plan page, comments received before Wednesday, Dec 2 go to the Denver Planning Board with the staff report, and later ones go to City Council (vote expected early 2027; Denver7 says January). The Broncos’ rezoning application follows adoption, and the team still targets spring 2027 construction and a 2031 opening.
