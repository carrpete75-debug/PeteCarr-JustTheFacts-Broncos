Record

5–22nd in AFC West · 0.5 GB

A half game behind Kansas City (5–1); nothing has been played since Monday night. ESPN’s standings this morning: KC 5–1 (143–103, AFC No. 1 seed), Denver 5–2 (144–141, No. 5 seed), Las Vegas 3–4 (2.5 GB), the Chargers 0–6 (5 GB). Denver is 3–0 at home, 2–2 on the road, 1–1 in the division, and has won three straight. Kansas City is 3–0 in the division and lost for the first time Sunday night, 20–3 at Seattle, with 213 total yards and no red-zone trips. Beat KC on Sunday and Denver goes to 6–2, a half game ahead, and splits the season series 1–1 after the 31–10 Week 1 loss at Arrowhead.

Lead news · Wednesday morning: Still no diagnosis or timeline released for Waddle (chest); today’s practice report is the first official read. Payton on Monday, asked about KC week: “I don’t see rivalry weeks in the NFL … I don’t see the Auburn-Alabama. I don’t see the Ohio State-Michigan.” He also said, “Quite honestly, we need to get [Marvin Mims] more touches on offense, and we’ll do that” (SI / DNVR). OLB Que Robinson called it “almost like a redemption game” (Gazette); the Gazette notes Denver has won four of the last six against KC since 2023, Week 1 included. National power rankings put Denver anywhere from 3rd to 12th (CBS 7, NBC 6, ESPN 8, Yahoo 12, per Mile High Report). On the KC side, Andy Reid’s only postgame injury note was LB Nick Bolton’s neck spasms (“he’ll work through that”), and Rice (hamstring) and RT Jaylon Moore (concussion) are working back this week.

Last game · Week 7 @ Cardinals · W 30–16

For one Sunday the Broncos’ offense found the explosive plays it had been missing. A week after putting up 168 yards against Seattle, Denver gained 377 yards on 56 plays (6.7 per play) against the NFL’s worst pass defense. Most of that came from three snaps: Nix’s 62-yard scramble, Harvey’s 52-yard screen and Mims’ 47-yard catch-and-run. Those three plays account for 161 yards. The other 53 plays averaged about 4 yards each. That’s still the story of this offense, but it took what Arizona gave it, and the Gazette notes Payton’s first two games calling plays averaged 196 yards.

The details. Denver led 6–0 after the first quarter on Lutz field goals of 48 and 43 yards, in a quarter with 10 penalties, five per team. Arizona tied it at 6 on two Ryland short field goals. Nix, who had 37 rushing yards in his first six games, found nobody open and took off down the left sideline for 62 yards, giving Denver a 13–6 halftime lead. Harvey’s screen made it 20–6 at 10:26 of the third. Arizona answered with a 7-yard Trey McBride TD. On the next drive, Nix hit Mims for 47, and J.K. Dobbins scored from a yard out on the final play of the third quarter, 27–13. Jonah Coleman, back from IR, was the most efficient back: 9 carries for 54 plus 2 catches for 15. As a team Denver ran for 159 yards at 6.1 a carry.

The defense bent but didn’t break. Arizona ran 75 plays, had 24 first downs and held the ball for 33:18, and Jacoby Brissett threw for 272 yards (27-of-48). McBride caught 10 passes for 111 yards and Denver had no answer for him. But the Cardinals went 1-of-5 in the red zone and 5-of-17 on third down. They settled for field goals on two first-half trips inside the Denver 10, and their last-ditch drive was stopped at the 1. Denver had 3 sacks (Singleton, Roach, Sai’vion Jones), 12 QB hits (AP) and 6 tackles for loss, and Alex Singleton made 19 tackles. There were no takeaways, so this time the defense won without forcing turnovers.

The problems: 11 penalties for 114 yards, a season high (Gazette), a 3-of-11 third-down rate, and only one TD in two red-zone trips. A team that does that against Kansas City won’t get away with it. Arizona has now lost 10 straight home games (AP). Denver’s 3–1 against the NFC West, and Arizona was the weakest of those opponents. What has to change before Sunday: clean up the penalties, finish drives (1-of-2 in the red zone isn’t enough), and find a way to stop the tight end. Kansas City just showed it can be held under 215 yards, but Seattle did it with an 11-of-16 day on third down that kept the Chiefs’ offense off the field. Denver went 3-of-11. That’s the gap to close. Yahoo’s power rankings (via Mile High Report) made the same point: take away the three long plays and Denver gained 216 yards on 53 snaps, about 4 a play. Explosives are earned, but you can’t schedule them against Kansas City.

New stadium news

newstadium.denverbroncos.com

No fresh overnight stadium item (checked Wed a.m.). Today, Wed Oct 28, is the last day for public comments on the Burnham Yard Small Area Plan public review draft (city release). Comments received by today are reviewed by city planners before the legislative process; after today, comments go to the Denver Planning Board (hearing later this year) and City Council (vote expected early 2027). The plan doesn’t rezone anything on its own; the Broncos’ rezoning application follows adoption, and the team still targets spring 2027 construction and a 2031 opening. The newest relevant pieces are still Bisnow’s Oct 20 food-hall report and Mile High CRE’s Oct 19 recap.

    Thrive Development plans a 14,700 SF food hall, a beer garden and coworking space at 1701 W. 13th Ave., north of Burnham Yard, targeting a Q4 2027 opening, years ahead of the stadium. The operator RFP is open until Nov 24.

  • Denver releases Burnham Yard Small Area Plan draft Mile High CRE · Oct 19, 2026

    Commercial-real-estate recap of the draft: five guiding principles, anti-displacement and affordable commercial space, and quotes from CPD’s Brad Buchanan, Councilmember Flor Alvidrez and Mayor Johnston. Calls the plan “a policy framework for future decisions, rather than a final development approval.”
