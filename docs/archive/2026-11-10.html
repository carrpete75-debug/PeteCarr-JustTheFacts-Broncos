Last game · Week 9 @ Panthers · W 26–21

Denver won this game the way it has won all year: not cleanly, not comfortably, and only because the defense got the last word. The Panthers had the better numbers. They ran 62 plays for 353 yards to Denver’s 312, averaged 5.7 yards a play to 4.8, rushed for 96 yards at 4.8 a carry against 82 at 3.0, and Bryce Young went 28-of-39 for 276 and three touchdowns. Carolina’s 3-of-3 on fourth down and 13 penalties for 119 yards (six coverage flags on the two practice-squad corners, per Klis) should have made this a rout in one direction or the other. It turned into a coin flip, because Denver handed back what it took.

The start was exactly what Payton drew up. A pass-interference flag on the first Delta Go call set up a 26-yard Lutz field goal, and Nix hit Sutton from 3 yards late in the first quarter for 10–0. Then the offense stopped. Nix’s second-quarter throw for Sutton was picked off by Corey Thornton at the Carolina 5 after a 48-yard drive, Carolina answered with a 14-play, 86-yard touchdown march, and a 48-yard Lutz kick at the gun made it 13–7. After halftime Carolina went 65 yards in six plays and Waller’s 15-yard touchdown gave the Panthers a 14–13 lead. Denver’s answer was a four-play, three-yard punt. The offense converted 6-of-10 on third down, yet it finished 2-for-4 in the red zone, and produced just 82 rushing yards.

The game turned on one play. With Carolina grinding 87 yards in 12 plays and set to go up two scores, Devon Key, the backup safety starting by necessity, snatched Young’s pass in the end zone (Denver challenged the original incompletion ruling and won the reversal). The Broncos then rolled 80 yards in 14 plays, and Coleman scored from 9 yards out at 10:17 of the fourth. The two-point try failed, so it was 19–14. After a Carolina three-and-out that lost 8 yards, Nix found a wide-open Bryant for 26 yards and the lead was 26–14. Bryant, back from four weeks on IR, led Denver with 4 catches for 56 and the score. Nix finished 25-of-37 for 237 yards, 2 TD, 1 INT, spread the ball to nine targets, and took only one sack. Coleman had 17 carries for 57 and a score, plus 4 catches for 31.

Then Denver nearly gave it all away. Young drove Carolina 64 yards in 12 plays, McMillan scored from 4 yards at 3:05, and it was 26–21. The offense went three-and-out for −10 yards, including an aborted snap that Coleman fell on. Carolina took over with about two minutes left. On the second play, Bonitto beat the tackle, stripped Young and recovered the fumble at the Denver 44 with 1:24 to go, and Nix kneeled it out. Bonitto and Ja’Quan McMillian each had a sack and two tackles for loss, Barron led the team with 8 tackles, and the Broncos won their 16th straight one-score game (Broncos Wire).

What has to change before Las Vegas: the run game is 27th in the league and the run defense 23rd (Klis), and Sunday’s 82-to-96 rushing deficit against a team missing four starters, including its LG and two linebackers is the number that worries. The secondary is the bigger problem. Denver played from the second quarter on without Moss on top of Jones, Hufanga and Abrams-Draine, and Jahdae Barron had a rough day against Carolina’s tight ends (Waller 8 for 90, Tremble 4 for 24 and a score). Waddle’s concussion costs Nix his best route runner. The bye comes at the right time. Waddle, Moss, Hufanga and Jones all need to come back on Nov 22 and not drift into the Nov 27 short week at Pittsburgh.
