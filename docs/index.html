The Broncos briefing

Final in Glendale: Broncos 30, Cardinals 16. Denver is 5–2, has won three straight, and sits one game behind Kansas City (5–0), which plays at Seattle tonight. Bo Nix ran 62 yards for a touchdown, the longest QB run in franchise history, and threw a 52-yard screen TD to RJ Harvey. Denver piled up 377 yards and 6.7 per play, without Pat Surtain II (inactive for the third straight game). The bad news came late: Jaylen Waddle (chest) and Zach Allen (hamstring) got hurt in the fourth quarter and didn’t return. The Raiders lost 35–21 to the Rams and fell to 3–4. Early Week 8 line: KC −1.5, total 42.5; Kalshi has Denver at 47¢.

Record

5–22nd in AFC West

One game behind Kansas City (5–0), which plays at Seattle on NBC tonight at 6:20 PM MDT. That game hadn’t kicked off when this page was published (~6:15 PM MDT); Kalshi had Seattle at 59¢ and KC at 42¢ pregame. Las Vegas lost 35–21 to the Rams at home, its fourth straight loss, and fell to 3–4. The Chargers (0–6) had a bye. Denver is now 3–0 at home, 2–2 on the road, 1–1 in the division, and +3 in point differential (144–141), up from −11. That’s three straight wins and five in the last six. Next Sunday the Chiefs, who beat Denver 31–10 in Week 1, come to Empower Field.

Lead news · Denver wins 30–16: Nix went 15-of-29 for 222 yards and a TD with no turnovers, and added 7 carries for 73 yards. His 62-yard TD run broke a 6–6 tie with 3:43 left in the half, and the Gazette calls it the longest run by a Broncos QB in the team’s 67 years. Harvey’s 52-yard screen TD and Mims’ 47-yard catch were, along with the Nix run, three of Denver’s four longest plays of the season (PFT). Surtain sat for the third straight game. Waddle (chest) and Allen (hamstring) left late in the fourth quarter and didn’t return (PFT). Malcolm Roach was checked for a concussion and returned (AP). Lutz made all three field goals and all three PATs, and his streak is now 111 straight PATs, one short of Justin Tucker’s post-2015 record (Gazette).

Last game · Week 7 @ Cardinals · W 30–16

For one Sunday the Broncos’ offense found the explosive plays it had been missing. A week after putting up 168 yards against Seattle, Denver gained 377 yards on 56 plays (6.7 per play) against the NFL’s worst pass defense. Most of that came from three snaps: Nix’s 62-yard scramble, Harvey’s 52-yard screen and Mims’ 47-yard catch-and-run. Those three plays account for 161 yards. The other 53 plays averaged about 4 yards each. That’s still the story of this offense, but it took what Arizona gave it, and the Gazette notes Payton’s first two games calling plays averaged 196 yards.

The details. Denver led 6–0 after the first quarter on Lutz field goals of 48 and 43 yards, in a quarter with 10 penalties, five per team. Arizona tied it at 6 on two Ryland short field goals. Nix, who had 37 rushing yards in his first six games, found nobody open and took off down the left sideline for 62 yards, giving Denver a 13–6 halftime lead. Harvey’s screen made it 20–6 at 10:26 of the third. Arizona answered with a 7-yard Trey McBride TD. On the next drive, Nix hit Mims for 47, and J.K. Dobbins scored from a yard out on the final play of the third quarter, 27–13. Jonah Coleman, back from IR, was the most efficient back: 9 carries for 54 plus 2 catches for 15. As a team Denver ran for 159 yards at 6.1 a carry.

The defense bent but didn’t break. Arizona ran 75 plays, had 24 first downs and held the ball for 33:18, and Jacoby Brissett threw for 272 yards (27-of-48). McBride caught 10 passes for 111 yards and Denver had no answer for him. But the Cardinals went 1-of-5 in the red zone and 5-of-17 on third down. They settled for field goals on two first-half trips inside the Denver 10, and their last-ditch drive was stopped at the 1. Denver had 3 sacks (Singleton, Roach, Sai’vion Jones), 12 QB hits (AP) and 6 tackles for loss, and Alex Singleton made 19 tackles. There were no takeaways, so this time the defense won without forcing turnovers.

The problems: 11 penalties for 114 yards, a season high (Gazette), a 3-of-11 third-down rate, and only one TD in two red-zone trips. A team that does that against Kansas City won’t get away with it. Arizona has now lost 10 straight home games (AP). Denver’s 3–1 against the NFC West, and Arizona was the weakest of those opponents. The real test is next Sunday, possibly without Waddle and Allen.

New stadium news

newstadium.denverbroncos.com

No fresh stadium item today (checked Sun evening). Nothing new on Burnham Yard or the stadium since the Oct 19 Denver Business Journal explainer. The live item is still the Burnham Yard Small Area Plan public review draft. Comments are due Wed Oct 28, three days from now (city release). After that, the plan goes to the Denver Planning Board (review later this year) and City Council (vote expected early 2027). The plan doesn’t rezone anything on its own; the Broncos’ rezoning application follows adoption, and the team still targets spring 2027 construction and a 2031 opening.
