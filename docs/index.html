The Broncos briefing

Denver beat Las Vegas 38–24 at Empower Field on Sunday to reach 8–2, its sixth straight win, and the Broncos are level with Kansas City (34–7 winner over Arizona) at 8–2 atop the AFC West. ESPN’s standings now list Denver first and seed it No. 2; both teams are 3–1 in the division with the head-to-head split 1–1, and ESPN does not spell out the tiebreak step. Denver led 17–0, watched Las Vegas tie it 17–17 in the third quarter, then outscored Las Vegas 21–7 the rest of the way. Waddle (3 for 61) and Hufanga (4 tackles) played; S Brandon Jones (hamstring) sat, and DE Sai’vion Jones left late with an unspecified injury. The Broncos honored Tom Jackson, who died Friday at 75, with a game ball. Next is Friday, Nov 27 at Pittsburgh (6–4, lost two straight), 1:00 PM MST on Prime Video. DraftKings has DEN −3, ML −156 / +132, total 37.5; Kalshi has Denver at 59¢ and the division at DEN 57¢ / KC 45¢. NWS shows light rain (about 55% chance) with a 21 mph west wind at the 3 PM ET kickoff. Kansas City plays Thursday night at Buffalo, so its result will be known before Denver kicks. The Henley trade was never finalized (correction retained below).

Last game · Week 11 vs Raiders · W 38–24

Denver won by two touchdowns and still lost the box score in the places that usually decide games. The Raiders out-gained the Broncos 370 yards to 351, averaged 5.6 yards a play to 5.0, ran for 6.0 yards a carry (143 yards on 24) against 3.9 (133 on 34), and Fernando Mendoza, the rookie making his first road start, threw for 267 yards and three touchdowns. Las Vegas also converted 8 of 18 on third down to Denver’s 6 of 13. That is not a team that got dominated. It is a team that was undone by sacks, flags and one catastrophic ending, and Denver’s defense and special teams earned every bit of that.

The first half was Denver’s best of the season. Wil Lutz opened with a 38-yard field goal, Bo Nix hit Adam Trautman from a yard out, and Coleman punched in a 1-yard score to make it 17–0 with 10:35 left in the second quarter. Special teams helped on the field-position side: AP reports two of AJ Cole’s punts were blocked. A flea-flicker for 36 yards to Marvin Mims Jr. that came up just short of the goal line was the longest play of the day. Then it leaked. Matt Gay kicked a 28-yard field goal, and with 2:45 left in the half Ashton Jeanty slipped past linebacker Que Robinson on a wheel route for a 68-yard touchdown (he finished with 5 catches for 90 yards plus 11 carries for 79, including a 46-yarder). Nix’s Hail Mary to end the half was intercepted, and he cracked afterward that it “should be like basketball where if you chuck it from half court it doesn’t count.”

The game turned in the third quarter. Payton called a quarterback sneak on fourth-and-1 from Denver’s own 39, Maxx Crosby and Folorunso Fatukasi stuffed Nix, and Mendoza hit tight end Ian Thomas for a 19-yard touchdown to tie it 17–17. Payton: “I don’t regret the call.” Denver’s answer was the grind it has leaned on all season: Coleman scored from 4 yards with 0:20 left in the third and from 7 yards with 9:21 to play, and he finished 24 carries, 111 yards, 3 TD, a season high for the rookie. Nix was efficient in the red zone (Denver went 4 for 4 inside the 20 to Las Vegas’s 2 for 3) but not much else: 19 of 35 for 225 yards, 1 TD, 1 INT, 6.4 yards per attempt, QBR 41.0 (ESPN), with only six rushes for 5 yards of his own. Evan Engram had a season-best 6 catches for 74 yards on 8 targets; Waddle, back from his concussion, caught 3 for 61.

Then the defense finished it. Denver sacked Mendoza five times for 40 yards (Alex Singleton had one, along with 11 tackles; AP counts 10 total QB hits), broke up seven passes, and was flagged only twice for 10 yards to Las Vegas’s nine flags for 63. With Bech’s 7-yard touchdown cutting it to 31–24 at 4:29, Mendoza dropped back on third down with 1:52 left; McMillian read the three-step inside route, reached back with his left hand for a one-handed pick, and ran it back 35 yards for the touchdown, the third pick-six of his career since the start of 2024 (Broncos.com). The 38 points included 7 from the defense, and the only turnover Denver gave up was the Hail Mary.

What has to change before Pittsburgh on a short week: the passing game. Nix averaged 6.4 yards an attempt against a Raiders defense that had Mendoza under siege, and the offense needed a 36-yard flea-flicker to produce its longest play. Denver also allowed a 68-yard wheel route and a 46-yard run on defense; the Steelers have Aaron Rodgers, who was sacked five times in Sunday’s 19–16 loss at Philadelphia, so Denver’s front should have chances, but a 17–0 lead cannot become 17–17 again against a team that can punish those mistakes. The positive for Payton: six wins in a row since he took the play-calling back, and a defense that is finishing games.
