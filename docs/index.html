The Broncos briefing

Denver beat the Chargers 23–17 at SoFi and is 3–2, 2nd in the AFC West, a game behind idle Kansas City (4–0) and level with Las Vegas (3–2), which lost 45–17 at New England. The offense managed 224 yards and went 1-of-12 on third down. The defense won it: 5 sacks, 3 takeaways, and a fourth-and-1 stuff of Justin Herbert that set up the 13-point fourth quarter. Short week: defending champion Seattle (3–2) comes to Mile High on Thursday. Injuries: S Talanoa Hufanga (ankle) left in the third quarter and was in a walking boot after the game; asked whether he can play Thursday, he said “I don’t know.” Surtain and Moss stay off IR. DK has SEA −3, total 41.5, ML SEA −155 / DEN +130. Kalshi: DEN 40¢ (−5) / SEA 61¢ (+5).

Last game · Week 5 @ Chargers · W 23–17

The defense won this game, and the offense almost gave it back. Denver led 10–3 at the half, already more than the 3 first-half points it scored in its previous two games combined, and the touchdown was a gift. On the Chargers’ opening series, Talanoa Hufanga punched the ball out of Charlie Kolar’s hands and Brandon Jones recovered at the LAC 40. Four plays later, after a Chargers face-mask penalty, J.K. Dobbins scored from 2 yards out for his first rushing TD of the season. A 14-play, 60-yard march ended in a 34-yard Wil Lutz field goal. Then the third quarter got away. Los Angeles, starting four rookie offensive linemen, went 73 yards in 14 plays and 9:28 to tie it on Herbert’s 7-yard TD to Hayden Rucci. With 1:15 left in the quarter, Bo Nix fumbled on a scramble at his own 40, Tuli Tuipulotu recovered, and Kimani Vidal ran 42 yards for a touchdown on the next snap. That made it 17–10 Chargers.

The offense was pitiful again, just in a new way. In Sean Payton’s first game back calling plays, Nix went 16-of-27 for 123 yards: 4.6 yards per attempt, a 27.6 QBR, and worse than last week’s 5.1. Denver was 1-of-12 on third down, gained 224 total yards, and scored on 2 of 5 red-zone trips. Six of its 18 first downs came on penalties. Two Chargers pass-interference calls alone were worth 46 yards. Evan Engram led the team with 4 catches for 55 yards. Jaylen Waddle caught 2 of 5 targets for 19, and Courtland Sutton had 2 for 18 plus the touchdown. The run game was the one thing that worked: 29 carries for 106 yards, with Dobbins at 18 for 68. Early in the fourth quarter, Marvin Mims Jr.’s 26-yard punt return set Denver up at the LAC 27. The Broncos gained 3 yards, and Lutz missed a 42-yarder wide left. Payton said it plainly: “We really struggled on third down offensively.”

The defense stepped up. With 10:33 left, Jim Harbaugh went for it on fourth-and-1 at his own 41, and Que Robinson stuffed Herbert for no gain. Harbaugh said afterward, “It was the wrong decision… Can’t think of a time where I’ve ever been more mad at myself.” Five plays later, helped by a roughing-the-passer penalty, Nix hit Sutton from 5 yards to tie it at 17. On the next Chargers series, Zach Allen strip-sacked Herbert and Robinson recovered at the LAC 23. Denver got to the 1 and then kicked a 19-yard field goal on fourth-and-goal (20–17, 5:07). It was the safe call, and against a better team it would cost them. D.J. Jones sacked Herbert on the next drive, and Brandon Jones intercepted him on fourth-and-6. Denver took two delay-of-game penalties on the next drive, the second on fourth-and-2 at the 13, and Lutz hit from 36 with 1:08 left (23–17). Ja’Quan McMillian’s sack ended it. Denver had 5 sacks (Nik Bonitto 2) and 3 takeaways, and Herbert finished 14-of-24 for 121 yards, 2.8 net yards per pass. That came against a secondary missing Surtain and Moss, with Jahdae Barron and Kris Abrams-Draine outside.

What has to change by Thursday: this was a winless team playing without both starting tackles, Derwin James, and Quentin Johnston, and Denver needed a fourth-down gamble, two fumbles, and an interception to beat it by six. Seattle is the defending champion and lost a 32–29 slugfest to San Francisco on Sunday. Going 1-of-12 on third down won’t beat the Seahawks. Neither will settling for a field goal from the 1, or a quarterback averaging under 5 yards a throw. On a short week, with Hufanga’s ankle in a boot, Denver has to stay on the field and get Waddle and Sutton the ball past the sticks. It can’t count on the defense to produce three turnovers again.

Postgame: Payton: “We took it away three times defensively and we forced a fourth down on a QB sneak. So essentially four big game changers.” Nix on the takeaways: “I don’t think we win the game without those turnovers… probably two of them we were in a good spot to score touchdowns and we didn’t, so we need to clean that up.” Nix called the play-calling switch “smooth” and “a collaboration.” Attendance at SoFi was 69,293.
