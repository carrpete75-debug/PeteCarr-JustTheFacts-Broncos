Last game · Week 12 at Steelers · L 5–27

Denver had the one thing it wanted handed to it, a Thanksgiving loss by Kansas City that opened the door to a two-game division lead, and then played its ugliest game of the year. The Broncos lost 27–5 and were outscored 14–0 by the Steelers’ defense alone. They finished with 205 yards on 69 plays, 3.0 yards a play, converted 4 of 15 third downs, gave up 3 sacks for 37 yards, took 6 penalties for 45, and turned it over three times. Pittsburgh, with Mason Rudolph making his first start of the season after the Steelers benched and released Aaron Rodgers, gained 247 yards on 47 plays and scored one offensive touchdown. That is not a close game that got away. It is a game Denver gave away.

It was over by the middle of the second quarter, and the first two possessions did it. Denver opened with three plays and a punt. On the second, third-and-15 from its own 19, Bo Nix took a shotgun snap on a designed screen to Jonah Coleman, got pressure from Nick Herbig and T.J. Watt, double-clutched, and fumbled; Watt fell on it in the end zone for a touchdown and a 7–0 lead (Denver Gazette). Payton: “I’m kicking myself for the first score.” Rudolph then hit DK Metcalf for 22 yards to make it 14–0 with 1:40 left in the first quarter, and Bleacher Report counted minus-14 yards on nine plays over Denver’s first three possessions, with no first down until the second quarter. Alex Singleton’s safety and a 44-yard Wil Lutz field goal made it 14–5, Chris Boswell answered from 47, and with 1:33 left in the half Nix threw deep for Jaylen Waddle and underthrew it directly to Jalen Ramsey, who returned it 36 yards for a touchdown to make it 24–5. Nix: “I saw him, just didn’t throw it high enough over his head.”

The numbers around the mistakes are not kind either. Nix went 22 of 40 for 169 yards, no touchdowns, two interceptions, plus the lost fumble, with a 44.7 passer rating, which the Gazette calls the worst of his NFL career. His last turnover was a desperation throw on fourth-and-21 from the Pittsburgh 30 with nine minutes left, behind Courtland Sutton and intercepted by rookie Daylen Everette. The run game did nothing to take pressure off: 26 carries for 73 yards (2.8 a carry), with Coleman at 15 for 26 and Cody Schrader 7 for 27. Waddle caught 5 for 53, Coleman 8 for 46 and Pat Bryant 4 for 42, which is a lot of short passing for a team trailing by three scores. Denver held the ball 33:52 to 26:08 and went 0 for 1 in the red zone; that is possession without production. Cold, gusty rain fell all afternoon, and Nix said the wind hurt the first few drives but added “I’ve played in worse.”

The defense was not the problem. Singleton had 13 tackles and the safety, Talanoa Hufanga 8, and Pittsburgh’s offense produced one touchdown (Metcalf) plus Boswell’s field goals of 47 and 33 yards; the rest of its points were the Watt fumble return and the Ramsey pick-six. What has to change before Miami: ball security (Coleman: “Coach said that ball security was a big thing for us, and we just didn’t do that”), protection and play design on third-and-long, and the run game, which Payton said left Denver “one-dimensional.” The Dolphins arrive at 1–9 and a 14.5-point underdog, so this should be a bounce-back game. Kansas City lost Thursday too, so the standings damage is one tied game, not a collapse; but Denver now has to win the race it was supposed to lead.

The Broncos’ stated targets remain construction in spring 2027 and a 2031 opening, privately funded, with a retractable roof (SBJ, Nov 13).

  • Broncos seek OK to exceed mountain view limit at Burnham Yard 9News · Nov 25, 2026

    Rezoning documents show the stadium could be as tall as 400 feet, needing an exemption from the 1950 State Capitol View Plane; the city estimates 180 feet is allowed before special permission, and the team is asking for up to about 220 feet more. Planner Brandon Shaver: roughly 1–2% of the stadium mass would need relief, and likely only a sliver of the mountain view is affected.

  • Denver Accepts Two Burnham Yard Rezoning Applications; Draft Sets 400-Foot Cap for Main Stadium Structure Front Range Development Review · Nov 25, 2026

    Denver accepted both applications Nov 23; the draft proposes a 400-foot maximum for the primary stadium structure, a 150-foot general maximum, and a 75-foot limit near protected districts. These are zoning ceilings, not a committed stadium design, and acceptance is not approval. The small-area plan goes to the Planning Board Dec 2.

  • RTD tells Council it opposes street-level rail crossings near Broncos’ Burnham Yard site Denver Politics · updated Nov 19, 2026

    An RTD board member told the Council committee Nov 18 that RTD expects traffic growth and worries at-grade crossings would hurt neighborhoods such as Baker; whether streets go over or under the tracks is still open and would be costly. The draft plan leaves crossings as “an on-going discussion” and says any change must go through a formal Public Utility Commission process; the Planning Board hearing is Dec 2.

  • Denver plans 2027 council review for 828-acre Burnham Yards plan The Badger · Nov 20, 2026

    After residents pressed at the Nov 18 session for enforceable anti-displacement, housing and environmental commitments, the plan returns to the Planning Board Dec 2 for a courtesy hearing and vote, with a Council hearing targeted for Jan 4, 2027. Staff said the plan is policy guidance and cannot make its metrics enforceable by itself.
