The Broncos briefing

Quiet Tuesday before a loud Sunday. Monday night (Dallas 38, Philadelphia 21) didn’t touch the AFC West, so the 5–2 Broncos are still a half game behind 5–1 Kansas City, and Sunday’s home game decides first place. The markets barely moved overnight: DraftKings still has KC −1.5, total 41.5; BetMGM’s AFC West price is flat at DEN +155 / KC −190; Kalshi ticked Denver down a cent to 48¢. The one real change is the weather: NWS now says rain showers likely after 11 AM Sunday (62%), up from 40%. Still no team word on Jaylen Waddle (chest); the first practice report is Wednesday.

Record

5–22nd in AFC West · 0.5 GB

A half game behind Kansas City (5–1), with Week 7 now complete. Seattle held the Chiefs to 213 yards, 11 first downs and a field goal in a 20–3 win on Sunday night. It was KC’s first loss, and NBC noted it was the first time in a Mahomes start that the Chiefs never reached the red zone. Mahomes went 19-of-33 for 165 yards and an interception; Kenneth Walker III had 14 carries for 36 yards. Las Vegas (3–4) lost 35–21 to the Rams for its fourth straight loss, and the Chargers (0–6) were on bye. Denver is 3–0 at home, 2–2 on the road, 1–1 in the division, +3 in point differential (144–141, ESPN standings), and has won three straight. In the AFC, KC and Buffalo are 5–1; Denver, Cleveland and Pittsburgh are 5–2, and ESPN’s seeding has Denver 5th. Beat KC on Sunday and Denver takes over first place in the AFC West, a half game ahead.

Lead news · Tuesday morning: No diagnosis or timeline for Waddle had been released as of Monday; the team has only called it a chest injury, the same area that limited him in practice Wednesday and Thursday last week (Broncos Wire, Gazette). Allen said after the game, “I’ll be fine.” Surtain, asked Sunday whether he expects to play against KC: “Possibly” (Gazette). The offense’s weak spot is still third down: NFL.com counts Denver at 18.9% on third down since Payton took back play-calling in Week 5, worst in the league, with an average of 8.1 yards to go. CBS is sending its No. 1 crew, Jim Nantz, J.J. Watt and Tracy Wolfson, and The NFL Today airs live from Denver Sunday (Denver Sports). Wednesday brings the first Week 8 injury report.

Last game · Week 7 @ Cardinals · W 30–16

For one Sunday the Broncos’ offense found the explosive plays it had been missing. A week after putting up 168 yards against Seattle, Denver gained 377 yards on 56 plays (6.7 per play) against the NFL’s worst pass defense. Most of that came from three snaps: Nix’s 62-yard scramble, Harvey’s 52-yard screen and Mims’ 47-yard catch-and-run. Those three plays account for 161 yards. The other 53 plays averaged about 4 yards each. That’s still the story of this offense, but it took what Arizona gave it, and the Gazette notes Payton’s first two games calling plays averaged 196 yards.

The details. Denver led 6–0 after the first quarter on Lutz field goals of 48 and 43 yards, in a quarter with 10 penalties, five per team. Arizona tied it at 6 on two Ryland short field goals. Nix, who had 37 rushing yards in his first six games, found nobody open and took off down the left sideline for 62 yards, giving Denver a 13–6 halftime lead. Harvey’s screen made it 20–6 at 10:26 of the third. Arizona answered with a 7-yard Trey McBride TD. On the next drive, Nix hit Mims for 47, and J.K. Dobbins scored from a yard out on the final play of the third quarter, 27–13. Jonah Coleman, back from IR, was the most efficient back: 9 carries for 54 plus 2 catches for 15. As a team Denver ran for 159 yards at 6.1 a carry.

The defense bent but didn’t break. Arizona ran 75 plays, had 24 first downs and held the ball for 33:18, and Jacoby Brissett threw for 272 yards (27-of-48). McBride caught 10 passes for 111 yards and Denver had no answer for him. But the Cardinals went 1-of-5 in the red zone and 5-of-17 on third down. They settled for field goals on two first-half trips inside the Denver 10, and their last-ditch drive was stopped at the 1. Denver had 3 sacks (Singleton, Roach, Sai’vion Jones), 12 QB hits (AP) and 6 tackles for loss, and Alex Singleton made 19 tackles. There were no takeaways, so this time the defense won without forcing turnovers.

The problems: 11 penalties for 114 yards, a season high (Gazette), a 3-of-11 third-down rate, and only one TD in two red-zone trips. A team that does that against Kansas City won’t get away with it. Arizona has now lost 10 straight home games (AP). Denver’s 3–1 against the NFC West, and Arizona was the weakest of those opponents. What has to change before Sunday: clean up the penalties, finish drives (1-of-2 in the red zone isn’t enough), and find a way to stop the tight end. Kansas City just showed it can be held under 215 yards, but Seattle did it with an 11-of-16 day on third down that kept the Chiefs’ offense off the field. Denver went 3-of-11. That’s the gap to close.

New stadium news

newstadium.denverbroncos.com

No fresh overnight stadium item (checked Tue a.m.). The newest relevant piece is still Bisnow’s Oct 20 report on a food hall planned next to Burnham Yard. The live item is the Burnham Yard Small Area Plan public review draft, and comments are due tomorrow, Wed Oct 28 (city release). After that, the plan goes to the Denver Planning Board (review later this year) and City Council (vote expected early 2027). The plan doesn’t rezone anything on its own; the Broncos’ rezoning application follows adoption, and the team still targets spring 2027 construction and a 2031 opening.
